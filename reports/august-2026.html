Executive Summary

August brought strong paper-price recovery and sharp premium compression.

August reversed July’s central dynamic. COMEX silver rose strongly, while Japan’s refinery and dealer layers followed higher and the street-retail market failed to keep pace. JPMI-AG gained 9.3% from the first August observation, but COMEX JPY/g advanced 18.8%. The result was a substantial normalization of the composite physical premium.

Market regime🟡 Moderately Elevated Physical Premium

The average JPMI premium fell to 10.3%, from 18.3% across the complete July dataset. Retail bullion remained above COMEX on most observations, but the extreme divergence seen in July was no longer the dominant market condition.

What changed

  • Average JPMI premium compressed to 10.3%.
  • Average street premium fell to 22.8%.
  • COMEX USD/oz rose 19.4% from first to last August snapshot.

What it means

  • Global silver repriced upward faster than Japan’s retail physical market.
  • RS and DB transmitted the paper-market rally much more directly than MSPI-B.
  • Mercari liquidity remained thin despite premium normalization.

Premium Analysis

The composite premium compressed from 20.2% to near 10%.

● JPMI premium %

The premium began August at 20.23%, fell to just 3.30% on 13 August, and finished the available month at 10.56%. The monthly average of 10.31% was materially below July’s 18.3% complete-month average.

Analyst observation. This is the clearest normalization signal in the August dataset. It did not arise because Japanese physical prices collapsed. Instead, COMEX accelerated upward while refinery and dealer pricing followed, compressing the relative premium. Physical remained more expensive, but the degree of segmentation declined.

Methodology & Notes

How the report is constructed.

JPMI-AG

A composite measure of observed Japanese physical silver pricing, combining refinery buyback (RS), dealer/pawn bid (DB) and qualifying peer-to-peer street retail (MSPI-B) when available.

RS

Composite of published silver buyback prices from major Japanese refiners, subject to availability and data-quality controls.

DB

Composite of observed Sv1000 dealer or pawn-shop bids, representing secondary-market acquisition pricing.

MSPI-B

Observed completed Mercari sales of qualifying one-ounce bullion coins, normalized to JPY per gram. Numismatic, novelty and non-comparable items are excluded.

Important limitations

  • This edition uses official observations through 28 August 2026. The 31 August business-day snapshot was not yet available at preparation time, so this edition is marked preliminary.
  • Mercari samples are small and can be influenced by coin type, condition, shipping terms and seller reputation.
  • Premiums are descriptive market measures, not guaranteed executable arbitrage returns.
  • All figures are research estimates based on observable public-market data and may be revised for data quality.
JPMI measures the price actually visible across Japan’s physical silver channels—not merely the international reference price translated into yen.

Conclusion

August normalized the premium, but not the liquidity problem.

July’s market was defined by persistent divergence. August was defined by convergence. COMEX and Japan’s wholesale physical layers surged, while Mercari street prices did not keep pace. The composite JPMI premium consequently fell from July’s elevated regime toward a more moderate 10.3% monthly average.

The most important proprietary signal is the split between price normalization and liquidity normalization. MSPI-B’s average direct premium fell to 22.8%, yet qualifying activity averaged only 1.84 transactions per observed sale-day and seven days were represented by a single sale. Japan’s retail physical silver market therefore became less expensive relative to COMEX without clearly becoming more liquid.

September will test whether MSPI-B catches up with the wholesale rally, which could reopen the premium, or whether increased supply and transaction depth confirm that the July dislocation has genuinely faded.
