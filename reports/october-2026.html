Executive Summary

The average Mercari premium rose from 23.45% to 29.30%. From September month-end, the street price declined 8.4% while COMEX fell 16.7% in yen. The premium widened as the benchmark fell faster.

Qualifying listings dropped from 2.00 to 1.62 per observed day. Ten October days had a single qualifying listing, up from one in September. The last five observations each had two listings, even as the street price remained lower.

3. Premium Analysis

Composite and street premiums over COMEX

The composite premium averaged 13.77%, up 2.60 percentage points from September, and ended at 15.50%. The street premium averaged 29.30%, up 5.85 points, and ended at 34.46%. Street premiums exceeded 20% in 18 of 21 observations.

The 62.28% street premium on October 19 came from a single Austrian Philharmonic observation at ¥475.00/g. Excluding that entire day in a sensitivity check brings October’s average to 27.66%, still 4.20 percentage points above September. The official series retains the day.

4. Market Layers

Refinery buyback and dealer bids finished 15.7% and 16.0% below September month-end, respectively. Their October premiums over COMEX averaged 7.47% and 4.53%. The 29.30% Mercari premium shows how much wider the spread was in the observed peer-to-peer coin market.

5. Mercari Retail Market

MSPI-B averaged ¥376.39/g versus ¥401.33/g in September, a 6.2% decline. October ranged from ¥324.00/g on October 7 to ¥475.00/g on October 19, and ended at ¥347.00/g. Ten daily prices were based on one listing, so individual prints could move the series sharply.

Git history retains the coin names and entered prices for all 34 submitted observations used here. Their daily arithmetic means match the published MSPI-B values. The submitted qualifying listings are the available raw MSPI-B sample. A separate Adjusted MSPI-B series would require a documented filter. A complete raw record would also need any listings screened out before submission.

The clearest overlap of falling prices and thin activity was October 14–16. In the final week, October 26–30, MSPI-B averaged ¥347.90/g versus ¥399.35/g in the preceding observed week. Each of the final five snapshots contained two listings. That makes the late-month pattern one of lower street prices with steadier observed activity.

7. JPMI Market Assessment

September’s outlook set two tests for a more durable street premium. October cleared the price test, with the average premium above 20%, while observed liquidity slipped below two listings per day. Even in the final week, when all five snapshots had two listings, the street premium averaged 29.66%. The October data show that the premium persisted, with limited evidence about how it behaves at higher trading volumes.

8. November Outlook

Watch whether Mercari sustains two or more qualifying listings per observed day and whether the street premium stays elevated when COMEX stabilizes or rebounds. Tracking both would help distinguish persistent retail pricing from spreads that widen mainly because the benchmark falls faster.

10. Methodology & Notes

The published October JPMI-AG rows match the simple mean of refinery buyback, dealer/pawn bid, and MSPI-B. Premiums use (Japanese price ÷ COMEX JPY/g − 1) × 100. The monthly averages give each observed day equal weight. The report uses the daily history’s COMEX benchmark for both premiums.

The final Mercari update per JST date supplies the listing count. The 23:53 UTC update on October 7 belongs to October 8 in Japan. Reapplying the JST convention to September reproduces its published 44 listings across 22 dates. Counts measure qualifying listings recorded in snapshots. They do not establish the number of unique sales across dates or total Mercari market volume.

Sources: JPMI daily index history · Mercari snapshot history · Archived listing inputs · Daily index calculation.

11. Conclusion

October closed with JPMI-AG at ¥298.07/g and a 15.50% composite premium. Japan’s street premium held as absolute prices declined, including in the final week when the sample stabilized at two listings per day. The ten one-listing days earlier in the month show why the premium and the qualifying sales count belong side by side.
