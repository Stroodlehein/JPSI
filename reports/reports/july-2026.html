Executive Summary

Japan’s physical silver market resisted the paper-market decline.

July produced a widening separation between internationally referenced silver and the prices observed across Japan’s physical channels. COMEX ended 2.0% below its first July observation, while JPMI-AG fell 5.2% as volatile Mercari prints pulled the composite lower late in the month. The more important signal, however, was persistent: physical silver remained priced above COMEX every day.

Market regime🟠 Elevated Physical Premium

Premiums were broad, persistent and most pronounced in the street-retail layer. The market was not uniformly tight—refinery and dealer bids remained orderly—but immediately available one-ounce bullion continued to clear at materially higher prices.

What changed

  • Average JPMI premium rose to 19.1%, from 12.4% in June.
  • Average street premium reached 47.5%, versus 41.3% in June.
  • MSPI-B ranged from ¥385 to ¥546/g.

What it means

  • Japan’s retail physical market remained partially insulated from falling paper prices.
  • Low transaction counts amplified daily MSPI-B volatility.
  • Refinery and dealer layers showed no evidence of disorderly dislocation.

Premium Analysis

The composite premium averaged 19.1% and never disappeared.

The JPMI premium ranged from 11.0% to 28.7%. The strongest readings clustered in the second half of the month, including 16–17 July and the 24 July peak. This pattern emerged while COMEX traded near its monthly lows and qualifying Mercari coins remained expensive.

July’s signal was not simply “silver became expensive.” It was that the Japanese physical market repriced downward more slowly—and less consistently—than COMEX.
Analyst observation. Persistent positive premiums indicate segmentation between paper price discovery and the local physical market. The premium is best interpreted as a composite of availability, distribution friction, retail preference and transaction-level scarcity—not as a pure arbitrage spread.

Market Layers

Refinery, dealer and street prices remained clearly separated.

Refinery buyback (RS)

RS averaged ¥326.97/g and ended almost unchanged from the first observation (-0.3%). The layer tracked wholesale conditions without exhibiting extreme daily gaps.

Dealer bid (DB)

DB averaged ¥315.74/g and ended 1.4% lower. It remained below RS, consistent with secondary-market margin and resale-risk requirements.

Street retail (MSPI-B)

MSPI-B averaged ¥451.60/g—well above both institutional buyback layers—and generated most of July’s composite volatility.

Layer interpretation

The orderly RS–DB relationship argues against system-wide physical stress. The exceptional premium was concentrated in immediately available, small-format bullion sold peer-to-peer.

MSPI-B was volatile because the qualifying sample was narrow. One or two completed transactions frequently determined the daily benchmark. Methodology & Notes

How the report is constructed.

JPMI-AG

A composite measure of observed Japanese physical silver pricing, combining refinery buyback (RS), dealer/pawn bid (DB) and qualifying peer-to-peer street retail (MSPI-B) when available.

RS

Composite of published silver buyback prices from major Japanese refiners, subject to availability and data-quality controls.

DB

Composite of observed Sv1000 dealer or pawn-shop bids, representing secondary-market acquisition pricing.

MSPI-B

Observed completed Mercari sales of qualifying one-ounce bullion coins, normalized to JPY per gram. Numismatic, novelty and non-comparable items are excluded.

Important limitations

  • This edition uses observations available through 29 July 2026 and is marked preliminary because the calendar month was not yet complete at preparation time.
  • Mercari samples are small and can be influenced by coin type, condition, shipping terms and seller reputation.
  • Premiums are descriptive market measures, not guaranteed executable arbitrage returns.
  • All figures are research estimates based on observable public-market data and may be revised for data quality.
JPMI measures the price actually visible across Japan’s physical silver channels—not merely the international reference price translated into yen.

Conclusion

July reinforced the case for treating Japan as a distinct physical silver market.

The month’s defining feature was not a dramatic rise in nominal silver prices. It was the persistence of physical-market premiums while COMEX softened. Refinery and dealer channels remained orderly, but one-ounce retail bullion continued to command materially higher prices and increasingly thin transaction depth.

JPMI-AG ended lower than it began, yet the composite premium averaged 19.1% and the direct MSPI-B street premium averaged 47.5%. Those figures show why a paper benchmark alone provides an incomplete view of the Japanese investor’s physical acquisition environment.

August will reveal whether late-July weakness in MSPI-B represents genuine normalization or merely lower prices within a less liquid market. JPMI’s base case is that physical premiums remain elevated until broader transaction activity returns.
